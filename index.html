    Assumptions

    Opening the page always starts from the defaults. Changes are not saved in the address bar; use Copy scenario link to keep or share a set of inputs. Real terms, pre-tax, unlevered: the IRR assumes no debt, so it is the project return before leverage. Rating bands for the unlevered IRR: under 10% weak, 10–15% OK, 15–20% good, 20–25% very good, 25% and up great. All capex in year 0, operation in years 1 to lifetime. Methane in MWh (LHV, 13.89 MWh/t). CO₂ and H₂ use are stoichiometric (CO₂ + 4 H₂ → CH₄ + 2 H₂O). Equipment costs are entered for a unit running 24/7 and each unit is then sized by its own uptime: the electrolyzer by the capacity factor, the methanation unit by the methanation uptime (never below the capacity factor). When methanation runs more hours than the electrolyzer, an H₂ buffer covers the gap; it is sized at the buffer size factor × an average day of H₂ × (1 − capacity factor / methanation uptime). H₂ plant capex is the stack plus the H₂ systems (power supply, gas separation and purification, compressor, water treatment and cooling, installation), each in $/kW of electrolyzer. H₂ leaves the stacks at atmospheric pressure and one compressor lifts it to the buffer pressure, or straight to the methanation pressure when there is no buffer. Compressor cost and compression energy are entered for 30 bar and scale with the delivery pressure (energy with ln of the pressure, cost with that to the power 0.7). The buffer can only be drawn down to the methanation pressure plus 2 bar, so the tank is sized, and paid for, at usable H₂ ÷ (1 − (methanation pressure + 2) ÷ buffer pressure). Methanation capex is entered for a 10 bar unit: 45% of it (reactor, heat exchangers, piping) scales with (10 ÷ pressure)^0.3 and a pressure-rating factor of 1.2% per bar above 5 bar, a rough rule, not a quote. CO₂ compression is not costed separately, and compressing the methane for delivery sits in Logistics. Solar output is modelled as a sine wave over the year (the seasonal swing, automatically flattened as the capacity factor nears 100%). The page opens on Spain: a point near its centre (40.0° N, 4.0° W) on a single-axis tracker with the array at the default 1.7× the electrolyzer, fitted as described here and stored in the page, which gives the defaults for capacity factor, swing, buffer factor and clipped solar. Pick another place to replace it: the page fetches the last ten full years of hourly irradiance and temperature for that point, turns each year into DC output per kWp for the chosen mounting (single-axis tracker limited to ±60°, fixed tilt facing the equator, or east-west at 10°; isotropic sky, 0.2 albedo, 10% DC losses, −0.35%/°C), with the electrolyzer following the array one to one. Each year is fitted on its own and the ten fits are averaged. The capacity factor is the yearly mean; the seasonal swing is the sine amplitude that best reproduces venting on the real daily totals; the buffer factor is the smallest buffer that still delivers the full output in an hourly simulation, averaged over uptimes of 50–80%. It is a rough fit on ten weather years, not a bankable yield study. Solar data via Open-Meteo, CC BY 4.0: irradiance from the EUMETSAT CM SAF SARAH-3 satellite record, the dataset PVGIS uses, wherever it covers the point (Europe, Africa, the Middle East, eastern South America); elsewhere, and for air temperature, from ERA5 reanalysis (Copernicus/ECMWF), which can read 5–10% off. The status line says which was used. Place search: Open-Meteo geocoding (GeoNames) and OpenStreetMap Nominatim (© OpenStreetMap contributors). Picking a place first shows the fitted results of the nearest of about 330 stored points worldwide (2025 data, marked ~), then fetches the exact data for the point in the background and swaps it in. Fetched sites are saved in this browser. On summer days when the electrolyzer makes more H₂ than the methanation unit can take, the surplus is vented and the electrolyzer is enlarged to make up for it, so pushing uptime too high costs more H₂ plant. With Auto ticked, the methanation uptime is reset to the value with the lowest production cost every time an input changes; moving the uptime slider switches Auto off. Hydrogen cost is per kg delivered to methanation, after venting. All power is bought at the solar price, including methanation power outside solar hours and DAC power when the CO₂ source is DAC. With DAC as the CO₂ source, CO₂ comes off the electrolyzer in step with the H₂, so a CO₂ buffer of 5.46 kg per kg of H₂ buffered is added, and DAC power and CO₂ cost also cover the CO₂ vented with the H₂ in summer. Biogenic CO₂ is assumed delivered as needed, with no buffer. The H₂ plant is costed in USD and converted at the exchange rate. Stacks are replaced when cumulative run hours pass the replacement interval, at the replacement cost as a share of the stack capex. Solar oversizing puts more kWp of array behind each kW of electrolyzer: the electrolyzer clips the midday peaks, runs more hours and is smaller for the same output. The power price is per MWh the array makes, so clipped MWh are paid for and wasted (a pay-as-produced PPA); methanation and DAC power are not taken from the clipped share. For a site with exact hourly data the capacity factor, swing, buffer factor and clipped share are fitted for every oversizing from 1 to 3 in 0.05 steps; the stored-point estimates have no hourly data, so they stay at 1 and the default of 1.7× applies once a site's exact data is in. Show on a chart, next to the oversizing slider, opens a floating window that shows one day of the site's hourly output against the stack (used and clipped), every hour of the year as a heat map of stack load, and the production cost at every oversizing from 1 to 3 with all other inputs as set; without a fetched site it uses an illustrative profile. Find lowest cost searches mounting, oversizing and methanation uptime with every price, capex, pressure and finance input held fixed. Plant size is not searched: every cost scales linearly with it, so it does not change the production cost. Scenario links made before the stack and systems split carried one total H₂ plant capex; they open with the stack set to that total minus the current systems cost.
