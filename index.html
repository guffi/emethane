Assumptions

Real terms, pre-tax, unlevered: the IRR assumes no debt, so it is the project return before leverage. Rating bands for the unlevered IRR: under 10% weak, 10–15% OK, 15–20% good, 20–25% very good, 25% and up great. All capex in year 0, operation in years 1 to lifetime. Methane in MWh (LHV, 13.89 MWh/t). CO₂ and H₂ use are stoichiometric (CO₂ + 4 H₂ → CH₄ + 2 H₂O). Equipment costs are entered for a unit running 24/7 and each unit is then sized by its own uptime: the electrolyzer by the capacity factor, the methanation unit by the methanation uptime (never below the capacity factor). When methanation runs more hours than the electrolyzer, an H₂ buffer covers the gap; it is sized at 1.3 × an average day of H₂ × (1 − capacity factor / methanation uptime), a rule fitted to an hourly simulation of a tracker plant in southern Spain. Solar output is modelled as a sine wave over the year (the seasonal swing, automatically flattened as the capacity factor nears 100%). On summer days when the electrolyzer makes more H₂ than the methanation unit can take, the surplus is vented and the electrolyzer is enlarged to make up for it, so pushing uptime too high costs more H₂ plant. Hydrogen cost is per kg delivered to methanation, after venting. All power is bought at the solar price, including methanation power outside solar hours and DAC power when the CO₂ source is DAC. With DAC as the CO₂ source, CO₂ comes off the electrolyzer in step with the H₂, so a CO₂ buffer of 5.46 kg per kg of H₂ buffered is added, and DAC power and CO₂ cost also cover the CO₂ vented with the H₂ in summer. Biogenic CO₂ is assumed delivered as needed, with no buffer. The H₂ plant is costed in USD and converted at the exchange rate. Stacks are replaced when cumulative run hours pass the replacement interval.
